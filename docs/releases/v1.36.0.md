This release adds trusted networks, whose devices skip signing in, and keeps tapiOca safe when a door is left open by mistake.

## New

- **Trusted networks.** On the **Access** tab, list your home network (such as `192.168.1.0/24`), one device, or your own VPN. Its devices open on **Who's tuning in?**, where each person picks themselves (a PIN if they have one; an Admin always gives a PIN or password), or are **Signed in as** one person. Nothing is trusted until you add it, and an Admin signed in by a trusted network gives their password for Admin tasks. Works on tapiOca's page now, and in tapiOca's apps from their next releases (see [Trusted networks](https://github.com/egadgetboy/tapioca-server#trusted-networks)).
- **Trusted proxies.** tapiOca believes a visitor's address only from a reverse proxy or tunnel you list, never just because a device sends one, and each header it sends must agree.
- **Safe when a door is left open.** The home port forwarded on the router, a proxy pointed at 3310, forwarding headers from a device that isn't listed, or Docker hiding every device's address: each is treated as from the internet, or never trusted, with an Admin alert that never gives an address. With signing in off, tapiOca answers only by a home name (see [Doors left open](https://github.com/egadgetboy/tapioca-server#doors-left-open)).
- **New installs have everyone sign in.** Setup makes you the first Admin, and can trust your home network in the same step.
- **Tuner access by network,** such as `192.168.1.0/24`, as well as single devices.
- **There's always an Admin.** The last one can't be removed or made a User.
- **An Admin's PIN locked** by wrong tries is an Admin alert.

## Fixed

- A visitor a reverse proxy couldn't name no longer uses up the wrong-password limit for everyone through it.
- A sign-in made by picking someone with no PIN or password can no longer link an app.

## Updating

`tapioca-1.36.0.zip` is the source. Update as the banner, or [Updating](https://github.com/egadgetboy/tapioca-server#updating), says. This update adds two columns to the database, so tapiOca first copies it to `backups/before-1.36.0-<date>.db` in its data folder. Nothing is trusted until you add it, and everyone stays signed in. **Behind a reverse proxy or Cloudflare Tunnel? List its home-network address under Trusted proxies on the Access tab right away**: until then, everyone through it counts as one address for wrong passwords, and tapiOca's apps can't sign in from outside. With signing in off, tapiOca keeps working at home, and an alert suggests turning it on. It then answers by its address or a home name only: opened by another name, such as `tv.example.com`, it shows only its page until you add the name under Names for tapiOca at home. Setup asks **Who can use tapiOca** once more, to offer your network.

## Rolling back

To go back to 1.35.0, follow [Rolling back](https://github.com/egadgetboy/tapioca-server#rolling-back): stop tapiOca, put `backups/before-1.36.0-<date>.db` in place of `stationplay.db` (deleting `stationplay.db-wal` and `stationplay.db-shm`), and use the image `ghcr.io/egadgetboy/tapioca-server:1.35.0`. Everything returns to how it was before the update. Without that copy, 1.35.0 ignores trusted networks (their devices sign in), and if Tuner access lists only networks, any device at home can use the tuner until you update again, which brings them all back.
