Found by tapiOca

The rest of what tapiOca found: files you’re handling yourself, files no station or Media plays now, and files that can’t be shown correctly on the stations.

What’s on this list is off the air on every station, and other programs play in their place. In Media, in tapiOca’s apps, a broken file doesn’t play (another version of it does, if there is one) and a damaged one still does. Missing files are gone from disk or from Plex while a station or Media still has them. Broken files won’t play. Damaged files play, but the picture or sound breaks up. Unsupported files play but can’t be shown correctly (Dolby Vision profile 5). Replace them with another version. Fix or replace the file, then choose Retry. Or let the list clear itself: a program comes off the list when Plex has it again under a new ID (after a file is renamed, for example), or when it’s missing and neither a station nor Media has it anymore. (Removed it on purpose? Take it off the stations its entry lists.) A program with a new file, or one the quick check took off the air, comes off once it passes the quick check. Problems found by the deep scan or during playback stay until the file changes. Retry also tells the checks to leave that file on the air.

When people watch

A viewing counts once it lasts a minute, so flipping past a station doesn’t count. tapiOca counts the streams Plex asks it for, so several people watching one station together in Plex (or Plex recording it) may count as one viewing; each of tapiOca’s apps counts on its own. Stats are kept for 400 days. Today means since midnight.

Adding tapiOca to Plex
  1. In Plex, open Settings → Live TV & DVR → Set Up Plex DVR.
  2. Choose Don’t see your HDHomeRun device? Enter its network address manually (Plex can’t find tapiOca on its own) and enter the tuner address above.
  3. When Plex asks for guide data, choose Have an XMLTV guide on your server? Click here to use it and enter the guide address for Plex, shown above.
  4. Plex matches your stations automatically (it calls them channels). Continue, and they appear in the Live TV guide in Plex.

tapiOca keeps the Plex guide up to date on its own: when a station changes, it asks Plex to refresh the guide. If it can’t (see Guide updates above), refresh the guide in Plex after you change a station: Settings → Live TV & DVR → your tuner → Refresh Guide. For a new station, also choose Scan for channels there so Plex adds it.

Watching in Jellyfin, Emby, Kodi, or another app
  • Jellyfin: Dashboard → Live TV → Tuner Devices + → M3U Tuner, with the playlist address above. Then TV Guide Data Providers + → XMLTV, with the guide address for other apps.
  • Emby (needs Emby Premiere): Settings → Live TV → add a TV source → M3U Tuner, with the playlist address. Then add a guide data provider → XMLTV, with the guide address for other apps.
  • Kodi: Install its PVR Simple Client add-on (under PVR clients). In its settings, set M3U play list URL to the playlist address and XMLTV URL to the guide address for other apps.
  • Other apps (Channels DVR, VLC…): Give them the playlist address. It tells them where to find the guide.

These apps refresh their guide on their own schedule (about once a day). After you change a station, refresh the guide in the app to see the change right away.

Media folders

Every Media folder tapiOca has. Switch one off to keep it out of tapiOca’s apps; stations can still use it. Add a Media folder to have tapiOca read shows or movies itself, from the folders it can see. It takes each title’s details from the NFO files beside it, the file itself and its name, and its pictures from beside it, and reads them again every 6 hours, when you choose Read again, and when Sonarr or Radarr brings a replacement tapiOca asked for.

Picture and tuners

Backups

Every night, tapiOca backs up your stations, settings, the Broken files list, your logos (uploaded or from Plex), who can sign in, and the viewing stats. It keeps the last 7 backups in the backups folder inside its data folder. To keep backups small, Intro Bumper videos aren’t included; they stay in the bumpers folder. Restoring a backup replaces all of this with the backup’s copy, and tapiOca restarts to finish.
